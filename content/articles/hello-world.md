# Hello World

I've always enjoyed browsing the internet, talking to other people and commenting on their blog posts. I never expected to have one myself, but here we are.

So what is this post about? Mostly three things: what this website is, why I made it and why it looks the way it does.

## What is this website?

At its most basic, it's my portfolio: you can find some information about me and ways to contact me. But it's also a place where I can spew my own opinions and share any public projects I'm working on or tinkering with.

There's no RSS feed yet (it's a planned feature... probably... one day), so if you want to see what's new, check back here. Type `ls ~/articles` or `ls ~/projects`, or click **articles** or **projects** in the bar at the bottom. Both lists show the newest first. There's no search yet either, that's also a planned feature.

## Why did I make it?

Now isn't that a good question?

To be honest, the original version of this website was just a simple portfolio and a way to share my projects. The real reason it existed was that I had a domain and wanted to give a talk about [Leptos](https://leptos.dev/), a Rust frontend framework, so I needed something to demonstrate it with.

So why the change, and why move away from Rust?

After I built and deployed the old website, it just sat there and I never touched it again. Then recently I was checking out the website of my online friend [Vildravn](https://vildravn.eu/), saw that he used the [WebTUI](https://webtui.ironclad.sh/) CSS framework and got inspired to make a website that works like a command line. That gave me some fun ideas, but more on that in the next section. I also wanted a simple way to write articles like this one, so that adding more in the future is easy.

As for Rust, there's no really good reason. I still find Leptos an interesting framework, and a way to move away from JavaScript (or TypeScript, for that matter) as much as possible. But I recently started making some small projects with [Tauri](https://v2.tauri.app/), using [Vue](https://vuejs.org/) for the frontend, so I wanted to experiment with it and build something new that also makes these articles easy to add.

## Why does it look like this?

As I mentioned in the previous section, I got inspired to make a website that works like a command line. But why would anyone do that? Aren't portfolios meant for everyone, including non-tech people?

Well, yes. Anyone should be able to use the website, but at the same time a portfolio should be something personal, right? We might not have the crazy websites of the late 90s anymore, but we can still use this format to show our personality.

And that's what I did. I'm a nerdy, geeky person, I've always liked computers, and I'm an avid Linux user (I use Arch, btw 😉). That's the personality I wanted to show. But I also understand that some visitors might not be tech people, and that's fine. I tried to make everything, or at least everything important, available by clicking, while tech people can use the command line.

This also gave me the opportunity to play around and add some fun and gag commands, something for people to play with when they visit. There are even some secret commands and easter eggs for those who like to explore, or who wonder whether I implemented a certain command. I've tried to hint at most of them in some way.

## Closing thoughts

There isn't much more to add. The above should explain why this website exists in the form it does. (If it doesn't look like a command line anymore, I got bored of it.)

I hope this becomes my way of interacting with the world wide web. The way most of us are used to, social media, has become way too toxic for my taste, and this is my attempt at returning to something old with a modern twist.
